The problem

Most trading advice is never tested honestly.

Viral

Strategies spread on views, not evidence

Millions of new investors learn from YouTube and TikTok. The videos show winning trades. Nobody shows the 10-year record.

Tuned

Backtests are adjusted until they look good

Change the settings enough times and any strategy looks profitable on past data. It stops working the moment real money goes in.

Hidden

Trading costs get left out

Small, frequent trades pay a toll every time. A strategy can look great before costs and lose money after them.

What the data showed

Four things we learned from 25 tests.

Finding 1

Fast trading strategies lose to trading costs.

On the intraday strategies, the average profit per trade before costs was under 2 basis points (0.02%). Just crossing the bid-ask spread costs about 3. Even a perfect cost model couldn't save them.

Finding 2

Small samples lie.

One strategy made money on its first 53 trades. Tested again on 5 years of new data, it lost money in all 5 years. That's why every test here needs a second, untouched period before it counts.

Finding 3

Popular YouTube strategies didn't hold up.

A moving-average "AI trading bot" made money, but simply holding the same stocks made 2.6 times as much. Two popular day-trading setups lost money in both 5-year periods.

Finding 4

Slow strategies built on company fundamentals survived.

Buying after a big earnings beat and holding about 4 weeks beat the market in 7 of 10 years, in two separate periods. It isn't proven yet, so it's now being tracked on live data, with no money at risk.

The bigger picture

Some people do make money day trading. Almost everyone else loses.

Our 25 tests aren't the only evidence. Researchers have studied every day trader in entire countries, and the results tell the same story.

97%of Brazilian day traders who kept at it for 300+ days lost money
1.1%earned more than Brazil's minimum wage from it
<1%of Taiwan's day traders made reliable profits after fees, year after year
74–89%of retail accounts trading CFDs in Europe lose money

Why a few win

  • Skill is real, just rare. In Taiwan, the top 500 day traders kept winning the next year, earning about 0.38% a day after fees.
  • They have an actual edge, like faster information, cheaper execution or a niche others ignore, instead of a chart pattern anyone can copy from a video.
  • They manage risk strictly, with fixed losses per trade and daily stop limits, so one bad day can't wipe them out.

Why most lose

  • Costs add up. Every trade pays the bid-ask spread. Our tests found intraday edges under 2 basis points against roughly 3 basis points of cost.
  • They trade against professionals: banks, market makers and high-speed firms with better data and lower costs.
  • Short winning streaks feel like skill. Our stocks-in-play test made money on 53 trades and lost on 485. Many traders keep going despite steady losses.
  • Leverage makes losses bigger. European regulators restricted leveraged trading in 2018 because so many retail accounts lost money.

So, believe it or not? Day trading can work for a small, skilled minority. For most people, and for every popular strategy we tested, the data says it doesn't. If someone is selling a day-trading strategy, ask to see its 10-year record after costs.

How a strategy gets tested

Six steps, the same for every strategy.

Step 1

Collect the data

Minute-by-minute prices, daily prices with delisted stocks kept in, and earnings from SEC filings, all as they were known at the time.

Step 2

Verify it

The trading calendar is checked day by day (1,255 sessions, 0 mismatches), and gaps or bad bars are flagged before any test.

Step 3

Write the rules first

Every rule, cost and pass mark is committed to version control before any result is seen. No changes after.

Step 4

Test once

One run on 2016–2020 with pessimistic costs. Statistical significance is checked against luck with a bootstrap test.

Step 5

Replicate

Anything that survives gets one more run on untouched 2021–2025 data. Each period can only be used once.

Step 6

Track it live

Survivors are tracked on new data as it happens. The current forward test reaches its decision at 150 trades.

Under the hood

What it took to build.

Data engineering

  • Pipelines for SEC EDGAR filings and Alpaca market data
  • Point-in-time S&P 500 membership, with delisted companies kept to avoid survivorship bias
  • Versioned datasets with sealed holdout periods

Statistics and analysis

  • Pre-registered hypotheses with written pass/fail rules
  • Bootstrap p-values, Sharpe ratios, Holm multiple-testing correction
  • Out-of-sample replication and benchmark-relative returns

Cost and market microstructure

  • Measured real bid-ask spreads on 2,385 market minutes
  • Found the cost model charged 9.6 bps against about 1.0 bps actually paid, and kept the stricter one

Software and AI

  • About 14,000 lines of Python with 925 automated tests and 288 commits
  • A local web app with SQLite storage
  • A paper-trading bot running 24/7 as a background service
